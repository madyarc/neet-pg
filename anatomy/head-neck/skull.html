The Skull

Twenty-two bones: eight form the cranium, which houses the brain, and fourteen form the face. All but one are locked together at sutures; the mandible is the only movable bone. Turn the skull, tap a bone, then look at the sutures and inside the cranial cavity.

Tap a bone on the model or in the list.

Each kind of bone has its own colour; bones that touch never share one. Paired bones (left and right) share a colour.

A suture is an immovable fibrous joint between bones of the skull. Tap a suture or a point.
Sutures
Where sutures meet

Suture lines are where the bones of the model touch approximate; the points are found from where the named bones meet.

The cap of the skull is cut away. The floor of the cranial cavity steps down in three levels, from front to back. Tap a fossa.

Based on: Gray's Anatomy of the Human Body (20th ed.), chapters on the skull, each skull bone, and the exterior and interior of the skull; cross-checked with Wikipedia's articles on each bone, the sutures and the cranial fossae.
