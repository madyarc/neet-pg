The Femur

The longest and strongest bone in the skeleton. Turn it in 3D, look at its landmarks, see how a left is told from a right, what attaches where, and how its five ossification centres appear and fuse.

Explore the bone

Drag to turn, pinch or scroll to zoom, two fingers (or shift-drag) to move. Tap a name, or tap the bone.

Tap a landmark name to see where it is and what it does.

Landmark points are placed on a simplified 3D model approximate. Fine ridges (linea aspera, gluteal tuberosity) are smoothed on this mesh; their points mark where they run.

Left or right?

Three features fix which side a femur comes from. Switch between a right and a left femur and look at them.

  1. Upper end is the end with the head; the lower end, with the two condyles, is the larger.
  2. Medial is where the head points: upward, medially and a little forward.
  3. Posterior is the side with the linea aspera; the condyles also project backward, with a deep notch between them.

Hold it as if it were in your own thigh: head up and pointing toward your midline, linea aspera at the back. The thigh it fits is its side.

Five centres: appearance and fusion

One centre for the shaft, one for the head, one for each trochanter and one for the lower end. Step through the timeline and notice the order in which they join the shaft.

cartilage · secondary centre, not yet fused · growth plate · bone

Order of fusion is the reverse of the order of appearance: the lower end appears first (before birth) and fuses last (about the 20th year); the lesser trochanter appears last and fuses first.
